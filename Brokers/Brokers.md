# Which brokers operating in Italy in REGIME AMMINISTRATO accept ETF transfers from Trading 212?.

In Italy, Trading 212 operates under the regime dichiarativo (where you must manually report capital gains and taxes yourself), 
whereas brokers offering the regime amministrato act as a withholding tax agent (sostituto d'imposta). 

[1] (https://brokerchooser.com/best-brokers/best-etf-brokers-in-italy), 
[2] (https://finorum.com/investing-in-italy/)

While Trading 212 supports outgoing in-specie (portfolio) transfers for whole shares, 
transferring an ETF portfolio in-specie from a foreign broker like Trading 212 into an 
Italian broker operating under the regime amministrato faces major practical hurdles: 

[1] (https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/16105401083805-What-is-a-portfolio-transfer), 
[2] (https://quantroutine.com/brokers/best-broker-automated-portfolios/)

- Fractional Shares: 
Any fractional shares in your Trading 212 portfolio cannot be transferred in-specie and must be sold into cash first. 
[1] (https://finorum.com/best-brokers-europe-beginners-2026/)

- Custodian and Clearing Incompatibilities: 
Italian brokers with regime amministrato (such as FinecoBank or Directa SIM) use local Italian/European 
settlement and custody chains, whereas Trading 212 clears through external setups (such as Interactive 
Brokers-backed or specific nominee structures). 

[1] (https://community.trading212.com/t/issue-with-trading212-portfolio-transfer/84348), 
[2] (https://www.reddit.com/r/trading212/comments/ynlq3g/best_brokers_for_european_markets/)

- Lack of Universal In-Specie Inbound Support: 
Traditional Italian regime amministrato providers typically do not streamline or accept automated 
in-specie inbound transfers of foreign-held fractional or neo-broker UCITS ETF positions without 
requiring you to liquidate the assets or coordinate manual/costly counterparty transfer tickets 
(which are often unsupported or rejected for retail neo-broker accounts).

## What are your options?

1. Ask specific Italian brokers directly: Major Italian platforms like Fineco Bank or Directa SIM 
allow standard portfolio transfers from some traditional brokers, but they handle incoming transfer 
requests on a case-by-case basis and heavily discourage or reject in-specie moves from low-cost/neo-broker 
custody models. You would need to open an account with them under regime amministrato and submit a formal 
trasferimento titoli request to check if they accept the specific ISINs from Trading 212's custodian.

2. The Cash-Transfer Alternative: Most investors switching from Trading 212 to an Italian regime amministrato 
broker (like Fineco or Directa) choose to sell their ETFs on Trading 212, withdraw the cash to their Italian 
bank/broker account, and re-buy the UCITS ETFs inside the regime amministrato platform. (Note: Selling triggers 
a taxable event under Italy's 26% capital gains tax regime, which you must declare in your annual tax return 
under Quadro RT).
